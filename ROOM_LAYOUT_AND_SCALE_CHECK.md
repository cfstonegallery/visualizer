# Room Layout and Scale Check

Use this check before making a plain room image and again before adding tile or another repeating material.

## Why this check exists

A floor plan can be rotated, faint, or easy to read backward. A product picture can show one tile, several tiles, or one full mosaic sheet. This check turns those pictures into simple facts before they are sent to the image maker.

## Part 1: Make a four-wall map

Name the walls from the chosen camera view:

- Near wall: the wall where the viewer stands.
- Opposite wall: the wall facing the viewer.
- Left wall.
- Right wall.

For every door, window, cabinet, fixture, and piece of furniture, record:

- Which wall it touches.
- Where it sits along that wall.
- Which way it faces.
- Whether it should be fully visible, partly visible, or hidden from the chosen camera.

Also record what the doorway or camera lines up with. Do not rely only on words such as “left” and “right” when the plan is rotated.

Ask Chrissy to approve the four-wall map when the plan can be read more than one way.

## Part 2: Identify the exact product picture

Before using a material picture, decide what the picture shows:

- One individual tile.
- Several individual tiles together.
- One complete mosaic sheet.
- A room picture showing an installed repeat.

Record the maker, collection, color, exact item number, individual piece size, full sheet size, and the complete repeat. Count visible grout lines when they help show how many pieces are in the picture.

When a collection contains several products with similar names, do not choose from the color or shape name alone. Match the exact item number and flat product picture.

## Part 3: Do the scale check

Use the real surface measurement and the named product size.

- For a tile: divide the surface width by the tile width.
- For a wall tile: also divide the wall height by the tile height.
- For a mosaic: the exact full maker sheet controls the first scale check. Record which measured sheet side runs across the surface and which side runs front to back.
- Divide the surface width by the sheet side running across it. Keep the decimal result so partial sheets remain visible instead of rounding to a whole number.
- Divide the surface depth by the sheet side running front to back. Keep this decimal result too.
- Count the pieces across and rows deep inside one full sheet. Multiply those counts by the exact sheet repeats only as a second check.
- Use `visualizer-tools/material-scale-check.html` to make this sheet and piece-count check when a repeating sheet material is used.
- For a multi-tile pattern: count how many individual tiles make one complete repeat.

Write the expected counts into the image instructions. Examples:

- A 48-inch shower wall with horizontal 3 x 6-inch tile shows about eight 6-inch tile lengths across.
- A 9-foot wall with horizontal 3 x 6-inch tile shows about 36 rows because each row is 3 inches high.
- An 84-inch floor using 12 x 12-inch mosaic sheets shows about seven full sheets across.
- A 48 x 36-inch shower using a wider-than-deep 11 x 12 1/2-inch sheet shows about 3.84 sheet widths across when the 12 1/2-inch side runs across the shower, and 3.27 sheet depths when the 11-inch side runs front to back. If the sheet shows about 12 pieces across and 12 rows deep, the second check is about 46 pieces across and 39 rows deep before edge cuts and camera perspective.

## Part 4: Review the result

Check these separately:

- Correct product and color.
- Correct individual piece shape.
- Correct piece size.
- Correct full-sheet size.
- Correct pattern inside one sheet.
- Correct number of sheets or tiles across the known surface.
- Correct decimal sheet count and partial sheet at the edges.
- Correct sheet direction. Turning an 11 x 12 1/2-inch sheet changes the counts.
- Correct direction, offset, grout, and covered surface.

A picture can have the right color and still have the wrong size or pattern. Reject it if any one of these checks fails.

## Show-me-now rule

When the room, camera, main materials, and important sizes are known, Chrissy may ask to see a draft before every small appearance choice is answered. Make the draft at once using quiet, ordinary choices for the remaining minor details. State those temporary choices so Chrissy can change them after seeing the picture.
